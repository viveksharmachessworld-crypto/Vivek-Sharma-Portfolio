THE STORY BEHIND THE SCORESGOPALPUR · NAUBATPUR · BIHAR

A LIFE SHAPED BY CHESS

The first move
was his father’s.

From a village in Bihar to a return to competition, Vivek’s story has moved through family, discipline, loss, responsibility and a renewed ambition for India.

01 · THE BEGINNING

Gopalpur, Bihar

Born on 16 December 2002 in Gopalpur Village, Naubatpur, Patna district, Vivek grew up in a farming family. His father, Late Ravishankar Kumar, introduced him and his brother Vishal to chess and became Vivek’s first coach. Vivek began at a local rural school and later studied through Class 2 at Aswani Public School (CBSE).

02 · BEFORE SUNRISE

Hours no one saw

Vivek recalls training that often started around 3:00 a.m. and could continue from about 8:00 a.m. to 8:00 p.m. His mother, Veena Devi, supported the demanding family routine. Openings, calculation, tactics, puzzles, endgames, preparation and game analysis filled the practice days.

03 · THE JUNIOR YEARS

Village to state champion

Vivek and Vishal trained together for about five years and became multiple-time Bihar state champions across junior age groups. Vivek was Bihar State Under-13 runner-up, earned FIDE ID 45046328 with an initial rating of 1510, and won the Bihar State Under-15 title with 7.5/8 in 2016. His certificates also record 20th place (5.5/9) at the 2015 National Youth U-25 and 39th place (6/11) at the 2017 National Junior U-19. He later says he became Bihar State Under-17 champion.

04 · A DIFFICULT POSITION

When competition paused

Vivek’s account describes a dispute over advance financial support for selected players’ travel, accommodation and food, after expected reimbursements had not arrived. He says the request was refused, players placed below him were selected instead, and he was subsequently barred from competing. The matter reached the High Court; he says he later chose to pause the case after a discussion in which the Association’s side expressed regret. This summary presents Vivek’s account of a contested matter, not an independent legal finding.

05 · 2020—2021

Loss, responsibility, resilience

Vivek’s father, Ravishankar Kumar, passed away on 14 April 2020. Vivek says he stepped away from regular competitive chess during 2020 and 2021 to care for his family, home, farming and finances. He returned to selected events in 2022 and 2023; the two-year break was from regular competition, not a claim that he played no chess at all. He says chess’s habits—discipline, patience, analysis and calm under pressure—helped him rebuild stability. In 2023, he won the Diksha Bihar State Rapid event and the Blitz section, and finished runner-up in the National Amateur Below 2000. Vivek says he married Neha Kumari in 2026, as he began turning back toward his chess dream.

06 · 2026

A comeback, a larger dream

At the 4th Gujarat International Open Grandmasters Chess Tournament, Category B, Vivek scored 8.5/10 and finished runner-up among 313 players, earning ₹1,00,000 and a trophy. He now aims for the International Master and Grandmaster titles, hopes to represent India and dreams of helping bring a gold medal home. The story is still being written.

01 / CHAPTER

VIVEK SHARMA

FROM GOPALPUR VILLAGE, NAUBATPUR — TO THE DREAM OF GOLD FOR INDIA

A journey from a farming family in rural Bihar to the pursuit of International Master, Grandmaster, and the dream of representing India.

02 / CHAPTER

THE BEGINNING

Gopalpur Village, Naubatpur, Patna, Bihar Vivek Sharma was born on 16 December 2002 in Gopalpur Village, Naubatpur, Patna District, Bihar, India, into a farming family. His journey began far away from the major chess centres of India. There were no grand training academies.

No sophisticated facilities. No easy path to the national stage. There was simply a village, a family, a strong work ethic—and a father who believed that his son could achieve something extraordinary. That father was Late Ravishankar Kumar. And one day, he placed a chessboard in front of his son.

That first move would eventually change the direction of Vivek’s life.

03 / CHAPTER

THE FIRST MOVE

A Father, a Chessboard and a New Beginning Vivek’s early education began at a local rural school. He later attended Aswani Public School (CBSE), where he studied up to Class 2. During a summer vacation, his father introduced Vivek and his brother Vishal Sharma to chess.

What began as a simple game gradually became a passion. The passion became a discipline. The discipline became a competitive pursuit. And the competitive pursuit eventually became a defining part of Vivek’s life. His father, who had himself played chess at district and state levels, became Vivek’s first chess teacher, guide, disciplinarian and strongest believer.

04 / CHAPTER

BEFORE SUNRISE

The Discipline That Built the Player The early years of training were intense. Vivek and his brother would often begin their chess practice at around 3:00 a.m., while most of the world was still asleep. Their mother, Veena Devi, was the quiet backbone of this demanding routine.

She woke the children. She prepared their food. She managed their daily needs. She maintained the household around their training schedule. And she supported two young boys who were beginning to devote their lives to chess. The brothers could train from approximately 3:00 a.m. to 6:00 a.m., followed by their daily preparations, and then return to intensive chess work from around 8:00 a.m. to 8:00 p.m.

Their days revolved around: Openings. Calculation. Tactics. Puzzles. Endgames. Tournament preparation. Game analysis. Their father was strict about discipline. If training, routine or responsibilities were not followed, there were strong reprimands and strict consequences.

His philosophy was simple: Exceptional results require exceptional discipline. The trophies would come later. First came the hours nobody saw.

THE TROPHIES TELL ONE STORY.

THE HOURS BEFORE SUNRISE TELL THE REAL ONE.

05 / CHAPTER

TWO BROTHERS. ONE CHESS JOURNEY.

Vivek trained alongside his brother Vishal Sharma, who also went on to become a national-level chess player. For approximately five years, the brothers trained together, competed together and travelled together. They became multiple-time Bihar State Champions across their respective junior age groups, including Under-13, Under-15, Under-17 and Under-19.

Their progress was built on repetition, discipline, sacrifice and an unwavering family commitment.

06 / CHAPTER

THE FIRST MAJOR BREAKTHROUGH

BIHAR STATE UNDER-13 — RUNNER-UP

Vivek finished runner-up in the Bihar State Under-13 Championship. That performance opened the door to the national stage. He subsequently competed at the National Under-13 Championship, where his FIDE identity was established.

INITIAL FIDE RATING — 1510

For the young player from Gopalpur, this was the moment when the journey began to move beyond the village and into the wider world of competitive chess.

07 / CHAPTER

FROM VILLAGE TO NATIONAL CHESS

The progression was gradual but relentless: School → District → State → National Vivek played stronger opponents. He travelled for tournaments. He studied positions. He analysed his games. He learned from losses. He returned stronger after setbacks. And gradually, the name Vivek Sharma began to become familiar in Bihar’s junior chess circuit.

08 / CHAPTER

THE NATIONAL YOUTH STAGE · 2015

At the 24th National Youth (Under-25) Chess Championship, held in Patna from 12–19 October, Vivek represented Bihar. His certificate records 5½ points from 9 rounds and a 20th-place finish. Chess-Results independently confirms his entry under FIDE ID 45046328.

09 / CHAPTER

2016

7.5 / 8

BIHAR STATE UNDER-15 CHAMPION

In June 2016, Vivek produced one of the defining performances of his junior career at the Bihar State Under-15 Championship in Kishanganj. 7.5/8 — 1st Place The result established him as one of Bihar’s leading young chess players. The dream was becoming bigger.

But the most difficult chapters were still ahead.

10 / CHAPTER

NATIONAL JUNIOR · 2017

Vivek returned to the national stage for the 47th National Junior (Under-19) Open Chess Championship in Patna, held from 3–11 September. The certificate records 6 points from 11 rounds and 39th place. Chess-Results lists him among the event’s entrants; the placement and score here follow the owner-provided certificate.

11 / CHAPTER

WHEN THE NAME BECAME KNOWN

By his mid-teens, Vivek had become a prominent name in Bihar’s junior chess scene. Then came the Bihar State Under-17 Championship. Vivek became the Bihar State Under-17 Champion. At that point, the road appeared to be opening towards higher national competition and eventually international chess.

But sometimes, the hardest positions are not found on a chessboard.

12 / CHAPTER

WHEN THE JOURNEY HIT ITS HARDEST POSITION

THE MOMENT THE BOARD WENT QUIET

As Vivek’s results improved, a place at national competitions seemed within reach. He had become a Bihar State Under-17 champion, and chess was not a pastime to him: it was the work he loved and the future he was trying to build.

For a family from Gopalpur, Naubatpur, travelling to a national event meant finding money for transport, accommodation and meals. Vivek says that support or allowances expected for earlier competitions had not reached him. Before the next national event, he went to the federation to ask for financial help so he could take part.

In Vivek’s recollection, that request was refused. He also says that players placed below him were selected for the national opportunity. His father spoke with the officials and the family appealed again, but Vivek says they did not get the resolution they were seeking.

His father then took the matter to the Patna High Court. A court listing identifies CWJC No. 13363/2019 and describes a petition seeking to challenge a 1 June 2019 letter that debarred Vivek from chess competitions at national, state and district level. Read the court listing. It documents the case and the stated prayer; it does not by itself determine the wider disputed account described here.

Vivek remembers that period as deeply stressful. While the case continued, he felt unable to play at any level. The part of life that had always given him direction suddenly seemed out of reach. The silence after a game was hard; the silence where chess should have been was harder.

CHESS WAS HIS PASSION.

AND FOR A TIME, HE COULD NOT PLAY.

This chapter is told from Vivek’s personal recollection. The selection, support and federation discussions described above are his account; the linked court listing independently records the case number and the challenge to the debarment letter.

13 / CHAPTER

2020

THE LOSS OF HIS FATHER

On 14 April 2020, Vivek’s father, Late Ravishankar Kumar, passed away. The man who had introduced him to chess. The man who had trained him. The man who had disciplined him. The man who had travelled with him. The man who had believed in his potential. The man who had dreamed of seeing him become an International Master, and eventually a Grandmaster.

Was gone. The man who taught him his first move would not be there to watch the final position. For Vivek, life changed completely.

14 / CHAPTER

WHEN CHESS WENT OFF THE BOARD

But Its Lessons Stayed After his father’s passing, the family faced emotional, financial and personal difficulties. Responsibilities increased. Life had to be rebuilt.

Vivek says he stepped away from regular competitive chess during 2020 and 2021, while concentrating on his family, home, farming, finances and the responsibilities that had suddenly become part of his life. He returned to selected events in 2022 and 2023, so this was a two-year break from regular competition, not a complete absence from chess.

For a period, chess was no longer his first priority. But something his father had given him could not be taken away. The mindset of a chess player. During those two years, Vivek focused on family responsibilities while regular competitive chess took a back seat.

The tournament calendar was no longer at the centre of daily life. Selected events appear in his record again in 2022 and 2023, while family responsibilities remained his priority. The period also brought real-life challenges that made him stronger. He learned how to handle responsibility. How to remain calm under pressure. How to make difficult decisions. How to rebuild stability.

And how to stand again after facing difficult circumstances.

15 / CHAPTER

CHOOSING A WAY BACK

The court process moved slowly, Vivek recalls, and the uncertainty weighed on him. He was carrying the strain of a long dispute while trying to find a way back to the game that had shaped so much of his life.

Later, Vivek says, representatives of the chess association—including its then secretary, Gharamendra Kumar—called him to talk. In his account, the conversation included an apology and an expression of regret about what had happened. That conversation mattered because it opened a path to move forward.

Vivek wanted to play again. He did not want the case to become the rest of his story. He says he chose to pause his pursuit of the matter and turn his energy back toward chess and life. This was his personal decision after a painful chapter, not a claim that a court ruled on or resolved every issue described in his account.

HE CHOSE THE POSSIBILITY OF PLAYING AGAIN.

THE CASE PAUSED. THE LOVE OF CHESS DID NOT.

16 / CHAPTER

THE GREATEST LESSON CHESS GAVE HIM

Chess had taught Vivek much more than how to win games. It had taught him: How to think before acting. How to analyse a problem. How to remain disciplined. How to make decisions under pressure. How to communicate and behave with composure. How to accept mistakes.

How to remain patient. How to find another solution when the first one fails. And above all:

HOW TO FIND THE NEXT MOVE WHEN THE POSITION BECOMES DIFFICULT.

17 / CHAPTER

WHEN LIFE BECAME THE CHESSBOARD

When Vivek stepped away from competitive chess and took responsibility for his family, he began to understand the deeper value of everything he had learned. The chessboard was no longer in front of him every day. But chess was still shaping his decisions.

The calculation he had learned on the board became planning in life. The discipline of training became discipline in daily responsibilities. The patience required in long games became patience during difficult circumstances. The ability to analyse a position became the ability to understand problems before making decisions.

And the resilience developed through losing games became the strength to recover from setbacks in life.

THE CHESSBOARD WAS GONE.

BUT THE CHESS PLAYER WAS STILL INSIDE HIM.

18 / CHAPTER

FROM DIFFICULT CIRCUMSTANCES TO STABILITY

Vivek gradually began rebuilding his life. He took responsibility for his family. He organized his life. He worked towards financial and personal stability. He learned to balance responsibilities. He became mentally stronger. And eventually, he reached a point where life began to feel stable again.

This period taught him something no tournament could have taught him. Success is not always a trophy. Sometimes success is simply being able to hold everything together when life gives you a position you never expected.

19 / CHAPTER

A PERSONAL BELIEF

“Perhaps Chess Came Into My Life for a Reason.”

Looking back, Vivek feels that chess may have entered his life for a deeper reason. Not because everything was predetermined. Not because every event was planned. But because the lessons he received through chess became useful far beyond the board. He believes that if chess had never entered his life, he might not have developed the same mental discipline, stability and ability to handle difficult situations.

Chess helped shape the person he became. And when life became difficult, those lessons helped him protect himself, support his family and rebuild his life.

20 / CHAPTER

HIS OWN WORDS

“I often think that if chess had never entered my life and I had simply remained on a conventional academic path, perhaps I would not have developed the same mental strength and stability. Chess taught me to think before acting, to remain disciplined, to analyse situations, to stay calm under pressure and to keep searching for a solution when things became difficult.

When my father passed away and I had to step away from competitive chess, those lessons did not leave me. Even away from the board, chess continued to influence the way I thought, behaved, communicated and handled responsibility.

When I had to take care of my family and rebuild my life from a difficult position, I relied on the mindset that chess had developed in me. It helped me remain mentally strong, make decisions, organize my life and gradually bring everything into balance.

That is why, for me, chess has never been only a game or a career. It became a part of who I am. It helped me protect my family, rebuild myself and reach a stable position in life. Chess taught me how to play on the board. Life taught me how important those lessons really were off the board.”

21 / CHAPTER

STATE TITLES AND A NATIONAL PODIUM · 2023

Vivek finished runner-up in the MPL 10th National Amateur Chess Championship’s Below 2000 Open. Later that year, he won the Diksha International Bihar State Rapid Championship, listed first among 136 players over 9 rounds.

The owner-provided certificate is for the separate Blitz section: it records first place and 8/8. Snoop Chess also lists Vivek first among 100 players in the 8-round Blitz event. The two Diksha results are kept distinct so the certificate’s 8/8 is not attributed to Rapid.

His 2023 record also includes sixth place in the Bihar State Senior Championship. These results mark continued participation during years when family responsibilities had made regular competition more difficult.

22 / CHAPTER

2026

THE DREAM RETURNS

After years of responsibility and rebuilding, Vivek’s life gradually reached a more stable stage. He married Neha Kumari in 2026. And with a new chapter beginning, an old dream began calling him again. Chess. The dream his father had started. The dream that had survived his two-year break from regular competition.

The dream that had survived his two-year break from regular competition. The dream that had survived grief.

THE DREAM WAS STILL ALIVE.

23 / CHAPTER

THE COMEBACK

Returning to a focused competitive pursuit after a two-year break from regular competition, while continuing to balance family responsibilities, was never going to be easy. The chess world had moved forward. Players had improved. New generations had emerged. Opening theory had evolved. But Vivek returned with something that cannot be measured by a rating:

LIFE EXPERIENCE.

He had already faced pressure. He had faced uncertainty. He had faced loss. He had carried responsibility. He had rebuilt his life. And he knew how to begin again.
