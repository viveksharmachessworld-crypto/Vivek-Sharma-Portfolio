THE STORY BEHIND THE SCORESGOPALPUR · NAUBATPUR · BIHAR

A LIFE SHAPED BY CHESS

The first move
was his father’s.

From a village in Bihar to a return to competition, Vivek’s story has moved through family, discipline, loss, responsibility and a renewed ambition for India.

01 · THE BEGINNING

Gopalpur, Bihar

Born on 16 December 2002 in Gopalpur Village, Naubatpur, Patna district, Vivek grew up in a farming family. His father, Late Ravishankar Kumar, introduced him and his brother Vishal to chess and became Vivek’s first coach. Vivek began at a local rural school and later studied through Class 2 at Aswani Public School (CBSE).

02 · BEFORE SUNRISE

Hours no one saw

Vivek recalls training that often started around 3:00 a.m. and could continue from about 8:00 a.m. to 8:00 p.m. His mother, Veena Devi, supported the demanding family routine. Openings, calculation, tactics, puzzles, endgames, preparation and game analysis filled the practice days.

03 · THE JUNIOR YEARS

Village to state champion

Vivek and Vishal trained together for about five years and became multiple-time Bihar state champions across junior age groups. Vivek was Bihar State Under-13 runner-up, earned FIDE ID 45046328 with an initial rating of 1510, and scored 7.5/8 to win the Bihar State Under-15 Championship in 2016. He later says he became Bihar State Under-17 champion.

04 · A DIFFICULT POSITION

When competition paused

Vivek’s account describes a dispute over advance financial support for selected players’ travel, accommodation and food, after expected reimbursements had not arrived. He says he and his family requested support before travelling and that he was subsequently suspended or banned. The matter reached the High Court; he says he later chose to pause the case after a discussion in which the Association’s side expressed regret. This summary presents Vivek’s account of a contested matter, not an independent legal finding.

05 · 2019—2024

Loss, responsibility, resilience

Vivek’s father passed away in 2019. From 2020, Vivek says he stepped away from competitive chess for approximately four years to care for family, home, farming and finances. He describes relying on chess’s habits—discipline, patience, analysis and calm under pressure—to rebuild stability. Vivek says he married Neha Kumari in 2026, as he began turning back towards his old chess dream.

06 · 2026

A comeback, a larger dream

At the 4th Gujarat International Open Grandmasters Chess Tournament, Category B, Vivek scored 8.5/10 and finished runner-up among 313 players, earning ₹1,00,000 and a trophy. He now aims for the International Master and Grandmaster titles, hopes to represent India and dreams of helping bring a gold medal home. The story is still being written.
