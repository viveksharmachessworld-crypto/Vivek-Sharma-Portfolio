THE STORY BEHIND THE SCORESGOPALPUR · NAUBATPUR · BIHAR

A LIFE SHAPED BY CHESS

The first move
was his father’s.

From a village in Bihar to a return to competition, Vivek’s story has moved through family, discipline, loss, responsibility and a renewed ambition for India.

01 · THE BEGINNING

Gopalpur, Bihar

Born on 16 December 2002 in Gopalpur Village, Naubatpur, Patna district, Vivek grew up in a farming family. His father, Late Ravishankar Kumar, introduced him and his brother Vishal to chess and became Vivek’s first coach. Vivek began at a local rural school and later studied through Class 2 at Aswani Public School (CBSE).

02 · BEFORE SUNRISE

Hours no one saw

Vivek recalls training that often started around 3:00 a.m. and could continue from about 8:00 a.m. to 8:00 p.m. His mother, Veena Devi, supported the demanding family routine. Openings, calculation, tactics, puzzles, endgames, preparation and game analysis filled the practice days.

03 · THE JUNIOR YEARS

Village to state champion

Vivek and Vishal trained together for about five years and became multiple-time Bihar state champions across junior age groups. Vivek was Bihar State Under-13 runner-up, earned FIDE ID 45046328 with an initial rating of 1510, and scored 7.5/8 to win the Bihar State Under-15 Championship in 2016. He later says he became Bihar State Under-17 champion.

04 · A DIFFICULT POSITION

When competition paused

Vivek’s account describes a dispute over advance financial support for selected players’ travel, accommodation and food, after expected reimbursements had not arrived. He says he and his family requested support before travelling and that he was subsequently suspended or banned. The matter reached the High Court; he says he later chose to pause the case after a discussion in which the Association’s side expressed regret. This summary presents Vivek’s account of a contested matter, not an independent legal finding.

05 · 2019—2024

Loss, responsibility, resilience

Vivek’s father passed away in 2019. From 2020, Vivek says he stepped away from competitive chess for approximately four years to care for family, home, farming and finances. He describes relying on chess’s habits—discipline, patience, analysis and calm under pressure—to rebuild stability. Vivek says he married Neha Kumari in 2026, as he began turning back towards his old chess dream.

06 · 2026

A comeback, a larger dream

At the 4th Gujarat International Open Grandmasters Chess Tournament, Category B, Vivek scored 8.5/10 and finished runner-up among 313 players, earning ₹1,00,000 and a trophy. He now aims for the International Master and Grandmaster titles, hopes to represent India and dreams of helping bring a gold medal home. The story is still being written.

01 / COMPLETE STORY

VIVEK SHARMA

FROM GOPALPUR VILLAGE, NAUBATPUR — TO THE DREAM OF GOLD FOR INDIA

A journey from a farming family in rural Bihar to the pursuit of International Master, Grandmaster, and the dream of representing India.

02 / COMPLETE STORY

THE BEGINNING

Gopalpur Village, Naubatpur, Patna, Bihar

Vivek Sharma was born on 16 December 2002 in Gopalpur Village, Naubatpur, Patna District, Bihar, India, into a farming family.

His journey began far away from the major chess centres of India.

There were no grand training academies.

No sophisticated facilities.

No easy path to the national stage.

There was simply a village, a family, a strong work ethic—and a father who believed that his son could achieve something extraordinary.

That father was Late Ravishankar Kumar.

And one day, he placed a chessboard in front of his son.

That first move would eventually change the direction of Vivek’s life.

03 / COMPLETE STORY

THE FIRST MOVE

A Father, a Chessboard and a New Beginning

Vivek’s early education began at a local rural school.

He later attended Aswani Public School (CBSE), where he studied up to Class 2.

During a summer vacation, his father introduced Vivek and his brother Vishal Sharma to chess.

What began as a simple game gradually became a passion.

The passion became a discipline.

The discipline became a competitive pursuit.

And the competitive pursuit eventually became a defining part of Vivek’s life.

His father, who had himself played chess at district and state levels, became Vivek’s first chess teacher, guide, disciplinarian and strongest believer.

04 / COMPLETE STORY

BEFORE SUNRISE

The Discipline That Built the Player

The early years of training were intense.

Vivek and his brother would often begin their chess practice at around 3:00 a.m., while most of the world was still asleep.

Their mother, Veena Devi, was the quiet backbone of this demanding routine.

She woke the children.

She prepared their food.

She managed their daily needs.

She maintained the household around their training schedule.

And she supported two young boys who were beginning to devote their lives to chess.

The brothers could train from approximately 3:00 a.m. to 6:00 a.m., followed by their daily preparations, and then return to intensive chess work from around 8:00 a.m. to 8:00 p.m.

Their days revolved around:

Openings.

Calculation.

Tactics.

Puzzles.

Endgames.

Tournament preparation.

Game analysis.

Their father was strict about discipline.

If training, routine or responsibilities were not followed, there were strong reprimands and strict consequences.

His philosophy was simple:

Exceptional results require exceptional discipline.

The trophies would come later.

First came the hours nobody saw.

THE TROPHIES TELL ONE STORY.

THE HOURS BEFORE SUNRISE TELL THE REAL ONE.

05 / COMPLETE STORY

TWO BROTHERS. ONE CHESS JOURNEY.

Vivek trained alongside his brother Vishal Sharma, who also went on to become a national-level chess player.

For approximately five years, the brothers trained together, competed together and travelled together.

They became multiple-time Bihar State Champions across their respective junior age groups, including Under-13, Under-15, Under-17 and Under-19.

Their progress was built on repetition, discipline, sacrifice and an unwavering family commitment.

06 / COMPLETE STORY

THE FIRST MAJOR BREAKTHROUGH

BIHAR STATE UNDER-13 — RUNNER-UP

Vivek finished runner-up in the Bihar State Under-13 Championship.

That performance opened the door to the national stage.

He subsequently competed at the National Under-13 Championship, where his FIDE identity was established.

FIDE ID — 45046328

INITIAL FIDE RATING — 1510

For the young player from Gopalpur, this was the moment when the journey began to move beyond the village and into the wider world of competitive chess.

07 / COMPLETE STORY

School → District → State → National

Vivek played stronger opponents.

He learned from losses.

And gradually, the name Vivek Sharma began to become familiar in Bihar’s junior chess circuit.

09 / COMPLETE STORY

WHEN THE NAME BECAME KNOWN

By his mid-teens, Vivek had become a prominent name in Bihar’s junior chess scene.

Then came the Bihar State Under-17 Championship.

Vivek became the Bihar State Under-17 Champion.

At that point, the road appeared to be opening towards higher national competition and eventually international chess.

But sometimes, the hardest positions are not found on a chessboard.

10 / COMPLETE STORY

WHEN THE JOURNEY HIT ITS HARDEST POSITION

THE DISPUTE AND THE BAN

Coming from a farming family, national-level chess involved significant expenses.

Travel.

Accommodation.

Food.

Tournament costs.

According to Vivek’s account, difficulties arose regarding expected financial support and reimbursement for selected players travelling to national competitions.

The system required selected and top-performing players to receive support from the Federation/Association for travel, accommodation and food. However, according to Vivek’s account, players were often asked to first travel and compete at their own expense and were told that the amount would be provided afterwards.

Vivek and his brother had already participated in previous State and National tournaments, but according to their account, the expected payments had still not been received.

By the time Vivek became Bihar State Under-17 Champion, the financial pressure on the family had become significant.

When another National Championship required him to travel, Vivek and his family took a clear position: they would participate only if the required financial support was provided in advance.

The disagreement escalated.

According to Vivek’s account, this became the central reason for the dispute, and he was subsequently banned or suspended, seriously restricting his ability to compete.

The family believed that internal politics and the growing prominence of the two brothers also contributed to the situation.

Whatever the circumstances, the consequence was devastating for a young player at the peak of his junior career.

THE TOURNAMENTS STOPPED.

THE TRAINING DID NOT.

Even when competitive opportunities were restricted, Vivek continued studying and practising chess.

The board remained part of his life.

11 / COMPLETE STORY

2019

THE LOSS OF HIS FATHER

In 2019, Vivek’s father, Late Ravishankar Kumar, passed away.

The man who had introduced him to chess.

The man who had trained him.

The man who had disciplined him.

The man who had travelled with him.

The man who had believed in his potential.

The man who had dreamed of seeing him become an International Master, and eventually a Grandmaster.

Was gone.

The man who taught him his first move would not be there to watch the final position.

For Vivek, life changed completely.

12 / COMPLETE STORY

WHEN CHESS WENT OFF THE BOARD

But Its Lessons Stayed

After his father’s passing, the family faced emotional, financial and personal difficulties.

Responsibilities increased.

Life had to be rebuilt.

From 2020, for approximately four years, Vivek stepped away from competitive chess and concentrated on his family, home, farming, finances and the responsibilities that had suddenly become part of his life.

For a period, chess was no longer his first priority.

But something his father had given him could not be taken away.

The mindset of a chess player.

For those four years, Vivek faced life at the ground level—away from the chessboard and away from tournament halls.

There were no trophies.

No tournaments.

No rating points being added.

But there were real-life challenges that made him stronger.

He learned how to handle responsibility.

How to remain calm under pressure.

How to make difficult decisions.

How to rebuild stability.

And how to stand again after facing difficult circumstances.

13 / COMPLETE STORY

CLOSING THE OLD CHAPTER

The old chess dispute had already reached the High Court.

The case was already being dealt with through the legal process, but hearings and proceedings in Bihar were taking considerable time.

Vivek understood that continuing the case could consume a significant amount of time and energy, while he was trying to rebuild his life and think about his future.

During this period, the then All Bihar Chess Association Secretary, Gharamendra Kumar, along with his team, called Vivek for a discussion.

The matter was discussed directly.

According to Vivek’s account, the Association’s side expressed regret and said sorry for what had happened. There was also an acknowledgment of the mistakes that had occurred.

Vivek viewed this conversation positively.

Since the other side had come forward to discuss the matter, express regret and bring the old dispute towards closure, he felt that continuing the legal battle for years was no longer the best use of his time and energy.

He therefore decided to pause the High Court case and move forward with his life.

This was not surrender.

It was not defeat.

It was a conscious decision to pause an old chapter and focus on building the next one.

THE CASE WAS PAUSED.

THE LESSON REMAINED.

THE LIFE MOVED FORWARD.

It had taught him:

15 / COMPLETE STORY

WHEN LIFE BECAME THE CHESSBOARD

When Vivek stepped away from competitive chess and took responsibility for his family, he began to understand the deeper value of everything he had learned.

The chessboard was no longer in front of him every day.

But chess was still shaping his decisions.

The calculation he had learned on the board became planning in life.

The discipline of training became discipline in daily responsibilities.

The patience required in long games became patience during difficult circumstances.

The ability to analyse a position became the ability to understand problems before making decisions.

And the resilience developed through losing games became the strength to recover from setbacks in life.

THE CHESSBOARD WAS GONE.

BUT THE CHESS PLAYER WAS STILL INSIDE HIM.

Vivek gradually began rebuilding his life.

He took responsibility for his family.

He organized his life.

He worked towards financial and personal stability.

He learned to balance responsibilities.

He became mentally stronger.

And eventually, he reached a point where life began to feel stable again.

This period taught him something no tournament could have taught him.

Success is not always a trophy.

17 / COMPLETE STORY

A PERSONAL BELIEF

“Perhaps Chess Came Into My Life for a Reason.”

Looking back, Vivek feels that chess may have entered his life for a deeper reason.

Not because everything was predetermined.

Not because every event was planned.

But because the lessons he received through chess became useful far beyond the board.

He believes that if chess had never entered his life, he might not have developed the same mental discipline, stability and ability to handle difficult situations.

Chess helped shape the person he became.

And when life became difficult, those lessons helped him protect himself, support his family and rebuild his life.

18 / COMPLETE STORY

HIS OWN WORDS

“I often think that if chess had never entered my life and I had simply remained on a conventional academic path, perhaps I would not have developed the same mental strength and stability. Chess taught me to think before acting, to remain disciplined, to analyse situations, to stay calm under pressure and to keep searching for a solution when things became difficult.

When my father passed away and I had to step away from competitive chess, those lessons did not leave me. Even away from the board, chess continued to influence the way I thought, behaved, communicated and handled responsibility.

When I had to take care of my family and rebuild my life from a difficult position, I relied on the mindset that chess had developed in me. It helped me remain mentally strong, make decisions, organize my life and gradually bring everything into balance.

That is why, for me, chess has never been only a game or a career. It became a part of who I am. It helped me protect my family, rebuild myself and reach a stable position in life.

Chess taught me how to play on the board. Life taught me how important those lessons really were off the board.”
